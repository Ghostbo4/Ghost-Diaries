How I got here

The story behind the ghost — how a $7 account turned into a life on-chain.

May 9, 2024

It started on an ordinary day — sunny, a little hot, my little sibling's birthday going on in the next room. I was sitting with my cousin and my brother, half-listening to my cousin talk about a $200 bet he'd made on a token called Polkadot, while my brother kept mentioning an app called Farcaster, and words like "Ham" and "Degen" that meant nothing to me yet. He never explained them. He just said they were making him good money.

That day, my cousin talked him into making us both Farcaster accounts. An account cost $7 back then. I wasn't interested, but I got dragged along anyway — we handed over $14, split two ways, and let the apps download in the background while the birthday party carried on around us.

Learning the language

I picked a profile picture off Pinterest and stepped into a world that ran on its own vocabulary. People were tipping Ham, sending "eggs" back and forth through QR codes in something called Long, and stacking Degen — except I had no money to speak of, let alone enough to hold 10k Degen or 250k Ham. There was also Superrare: buy a $30 NFT, get an allowance in return. I bought one. It never paid out, and it's still sitting in my wallet worth nothing.

For weeks I did what a lot of newcomers do — followed people, followed-for-follow to get noticed, and honestly, it worked. I wouldn't recommend it, but it got me in the room.

The tournament

Then came Strawberry Runner — a three-day tournament with $120 worth of $Strawberry going to whoever finished first. I had school. I played anyway. First night: four or five hours, no luck, didn't even crack the top three. Second night, I'd already decided to give up on it, but my cousin talked me into a few more hours. He fell asleep. I didn't stop.

By some miracle, I hit first place. I went to sleep thinking I'd won, woke up to find I'd been knocked to second. So I went back at it — two days in, I was basically a specialist at this point — and clawed my way back to the top before the deadline hit.

$120. My first real money from crypto, plus a Strawberry NFT. I put $40 into Degen and the rest — $80 — into a tipping project called Farther that everyone was talking about.

The swings

Farther rugged. All $80, gone. Degen was the only thing that didn't let me down — 10k of it was worth $40 at the time, and over the next couple of months it grew into around $300, right before it got listed on Binance Futures and took off. A few months later, Ham and Degen both shut down their tipping systems, and everything shifted again.

Noice showed up next with the same core idea — tip people just by liking, recasting, or commenting. After a month or two, they dropped a big airdrop: somewhere around $900 landed in my wallet. It pumped, I sold in stages on the way up, and then watched it dip for months after. It's sitting at a $1M market cap now, and I'm down about 99% on what I left in.

The one that changed everything

Fey Protocol was next — anyone who'd liked a certain cast could join the presale, minimum $400 in. I didn't want to risk that alone, so I asked Ryan, someone I'd never met, living on the other side of the world from me, if he wanted to split it 50/50. He said yes. I still don't fully understand why he trusted me — I was messaging him from Afghanistan at 6am his time — but he did.

The next morning I had $5,000 sitting in my wallet. The biggest single payout crypto had ever given me. I messaged Ryan the second I saw it and sent his half over — $2,500 — and that was one of the best days I've had in this space.

Where I am now

That Fey Protocol payout was the last big one. Since then it's mostly been downhill, and if I'm honest, I've been procrastinating a lot. Things have changed in my life over the past few months, and I know I need to get back into the work — building, showing up, putting in the hours the way I did those two nights chasing a $120 prize. Ghost Diaries is part of that: a record of how I got here, so I can't pretend I've forgotten.
